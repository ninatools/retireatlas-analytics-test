United States

A large and diverse country where climate, housing, taxes and healthcare access can vary greatly by state and city.

Photo: Statue of liberty and nyc skyline by Gustavo Collins · CC BY-SA 4.0 · Image resized/cropped for layout

The United States offers many possible retirement lifestyles, from dense cities with public transit to warm coastal communities and dry desert regions. Costs differ substantially between states and neighbourhoods. Healthcare access and out-of-pocket costs depend on eligibility, insurance coverage and the services needed, while state and local taxes can affect retirement income and housing. These five places are examples for further research, not a ranking. Anyone considering a move should compare total annual costs, insurance, climate risks and access to the specific medical care they may need. Some US city cards also link to Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) facilities; these are not general public hospitals, and access depends on VA eligibility. Check the official eligibility rules before relying on them.
